*Rum Rum Raspberry — Lancy Zhao & Marty Scott*
*April 2026*

## Hook

A cozy apartment sim where you date strangers, tend your space, and slowly learn
that love hurts.

---

## Concept

Spent Blooms is a contemplative life sim about preparing for dates and living with the
consequences. You play as Nema, alone in a small apartment. Each morning a newspaper
arrives with personal ads. You choose someone to invite over, then spend the day making
the apartment worthy of them — wiping stains, arranging objects, choosing music, watering
plants, pouring drinks. Every choice is physical, unhurried, and tactile.

When your date arrives, they notice everything. They judge your outfit at the door, react
to the drink you pour, wander the living room reading your shelves and reacting to your
plants. Their affection — rendered as a growing flower — is the sum of your preparation.
Afterward, you trim that flower. The trimmed bloom joins a growing bouquet in your
refrigerator.

Over seven days, the calendar fills. Dates accumulate. Some of them go very well. Those
are the ones who stop calling back.

## Core Loop

```
MORNING
  └─ Newspaper arrives — read personal ads, choose tonight's date

AFTERNOON (Preparation Phase — timed)
  └─ Clean stains · arrange objects · water plants
  └─ Select vinyl · spray perfume · prepare drinks
  └─ Set the apartment exactly how you want it seen

EVENING
  └─ Phone rings — date arrives
  └─ Phase 1: Entrance — outfit, perfume, greeting, cleanliness judged
  └─ Phase 2: Kitchen — make their drink from memory or guesswork
  └─ Phase 3: Living Room — they investigate everything you placed

NIGHT
  └─ Date ends · flower left behind
  └─ Flower trimming scene (cut stems, score, add to fridge bouquet)
  └─ Sleep → next day
        │
        └─ [after the third date — the one with the strange flower]
              Souvenir appears. They don't call back.
```

## Key Mechanics

### 1. Physical Apartment Preparation
Every object in the apartment is a physics prop. You pick things up, carry them, place
them. The watering can needs to be filled and tilted. The vinyl record has to be extracted
from its sleeve and walked to the turntable. The drink bottles pour in real time with
layered liquid physics. The preparation phase is not a menu — it is the game.

### 2. The Date's Gaze
Your date notices specific things in a specific order. Phase 3 is an item-by-item reveal:
the date walks to each object in the apartment, pauses, reacts. A thought bubble
appears — heart, neutral face, or frown — while a multiplier popup shows how much the item
mattered. Items placed in prominent spots are worth more. The camera pushes in on each
discovery.

### 3. The Flower as Score
Every date leaves a flower. You trim it in a standalone scene using virtual scissors —
cutting angle, stem length, and condition all feed into a score. The trimmed bloom joins
a growing bouquet in the refrigerator — a physical record of every date you've had. Each
person brings three flowers across three dates. The first two are beautiful. The third one
is... different.

### 4. The Keyword System
Every character has preferences that surface as highlighted keywords in the newspaper,
dialogue, and item descriptions. Liked things glow pink; hobbies shimmer gold; dislikes
pulse grey-blue. The system rewards close reading and careful observation. Attention paid
to a personal ad on day one pays off in affection on day three.

### 5. The Mood Machine
The apartment has a living atmosphere. Weather, time of day, the music on the record
player, plant health, and air quality all feed into a single 0–1 mood value. This drives
the directional light color, ambient tone, fog density, rain particles, and audio mix in
real time. A stormy evening with a dead plant and no music feels different from a clear
sunset with jazz and a healthy fern. The date notices.

### 6. The Horror Layer — Woven In, Never Explained
Each person visits three times. You learn their preferences, refine your preparation,
deepen the romance. Their third flower arrives wrong — wilted, dark, thorned, off in a way
the first two weren't. After that final date, they vanish. They stop appearing in the
newspaper. Calls go unanswered. A personal item of theirs appears in the apartment: a
necklace on the counter, a ring in the kitchen drawer. Every player will notice people
stop showing up. The horror isn't hidden — it's unavoidable. What's optional is how deeply
you read into it. The game never explains Nema. The player decides what it means.

## Aesthetic & Tone

**Visual style:** PS2-era low-poly with warm URP rendering. Soft vertex snapping, affine
texture warping, and 2D dithering give the game a distinctly retro texture while the
lighting and color grading remain modern and intentional. Time-of-day shifts from bright
morning blue-white to golden late-afternoon to cool night blue through a single
shader-driven sky system.

**Palette:** Muted, warm. Cream walls, terracotta pots, soft greens, amber lamplight. The
PSX aesthetic keeps the game feeling hand-made rather than procedural.

**Tone:** 60% cozy / 25% mystery & fun / 15% creepy & sexy

The apartment sim is genuine and unhurried — methodical preparation, small domestic
rituals, the satisfaction of a well-poured drink and a clean apartment. The observation
layer is playful detective work: keywords, preferences, details that reward attention
across encounters with the satisfaction of a puzzle clicking into place. The romantic
tension is real — dates feel intimate, attraction is palpable, moments on the couch
linger. The rare moments of dread land hard precisely because the rest of the game earned
the player's trust. A strange third flower. An empty slot in the newspaper. A ring in the
kitchen drawer that wasn't there yesterday.

**Audio identity:** The record player is a core game object — vinyl selection directly
affects the apartment's mood. Original lo-fi and jazz tracks scored for each in-game
record. Ambient audio shifts with weather and time of day. Sound design is tactile: the
click of a bottle cap, water filling a can, scissors on a stem. Every interaction sounds
as physical as it looks.

**Visual references:** Sorry We're Closed (PSX aesthetic, warm domestic horror), Killer7
(mundane horror, unreliable perspective), Spiritfarer (cozy with emotional weight).

**Mechanical references:** Unpacking (spatial arrangement as storytelling), The Case of
the Golden Idol (observation and deduction), Grim Fandango (physical world interaction).

slice-of-life games, and games where preparation and ritual are the gameplay. This

**Secondary:** Horror-adjacent and romance players drawn to psychological dread, romantic
tension, and unreliable-narrator storytelling. Players who discovered Doki Doki Literature
Club, Omori, or Boyfriend Dungeon through word-of-mouth.

**Tertiary — content creators:** The hidden horror layer creates a natural "reaction
video" pipeline. The same dual-discovery mechanic that made Doki Doki Literature Club a
cultural event.

**Market position:** Steam's cozy/life-sim tag has grown 40%+ year-over-year in revenue
since 2022. The top cozy indie releases of the past 3 years averaged well over 500K
copies. Spent Blooms targets the intersection of three growing audiences — cozy, dating,
and horror — that no current title occupies.

## Why This Game

- **The preparation IS the game.** Not a menu between story beats — a tactile, physical
  ritual you perform before every date. The hours spent arranging the apartment are the
  hours the game is most itself.
- **A date who sees everything.** The judgment phase rewards players who paid attention to
  keyword hints. Every item in the apartment has potential meaning. Observation is the core
  skill.
- **Cozy, romantic, and unsettling in the same scene.** The horror is never delivered by a
  cutscene or a jump scare. It emerges from the same systems the cozy gameplay and romance
  run on.
- **The bouquet as a living record.** Every trimmed flower joins a growing bouquet in the
  refrigerator. Open the fridge and your dating history stares back at you — beautiful
  stems beside wilted ones, strange third flowers next to perfect first dates.
- **Two marketing cycles from one game.** The launch sells a cozy apartment dating sim. The
  post-launch discovery of the horror layer drives a second wave of organic content creator
  coverage — the same dual-wave pattern that made Doki Doki Literature Club a phenomenon.

## Proof of Concept

**This is not a concept pitch. It is a working game.**

30+ interlocking systems across 20,000+ lines of production code. Independent technical
audit: 8/10 for quality.

**What's de-risked:**

- Full dating loop end-to-end: newspaper → preparation → 3-phase date → flower → sleep
- Physics interaction: pick up, carry, place, pour, water, spray, snap — all tactile
- Drink-making with physical pour, magnetic snap, and delivery
- Record player: physical vinyl workflow (extract, carry, snap, play), album art display
- Book collection, paired-item puzzles, watering with 2D cross-section simulation
- MoodMachine: weather, time-of-day, music, plant health, air quality — all feeding mood
- Flower trimming: virtual cutting, scoring, grading, fridge bouquet persistence
- Keyword highlighting across all UI surfaces
- Phase 3 scoring with multiplier popups and particle reactions
- PSX rendering suite: vertex snap, affine warp, dithering, volumetric light
- 7 defined date characters with preferences, reactions, dialogue hooks
- Entrance judgment: outfit, perfume, greeting, cleanliness (4 sequential evaluations)
- Accessibility suite: 15 settings, 5 categories
- Save system, calendar UI, main menu, tutorial, name entry, outfit selection
- Playtest feedback with Discord webhook integration

**What the investment funds — content and polish, not engineering:**

- Scripted narrative arc for 3–4 characters (vertical slice), then full 7-character cast
- Horror payload: disappearances, souvenir accumulation, environmental dread
- Art asset scaling from blockout to final quality
- Audio: original soundtrack, ambient sound, SFX
- Convention demo build

The core loop works. The technology is proven. The remaining work is production.

souvenirs and strange flowers, determines whether it's unsettling background texture or a
full-blown mystery. That range drives replay and content creation without additional
content cost.
