A cozy apartment sim where you date strangers, tend your space,

01 — Concept

A contemplative life sim about
preparation and consequence.

Iris is a contemplative life sim about preparing for dates and living with the consequences. You play as Nema, alone in a small apartment. Each morning a newspaper arrives with personal ads. You choose someone to invite over, then spend the day making the apartment worthy of them — wiping stains, arranging objects, choosing music, watering plants, pouring drinks. Every choice is physical, unhurried, and tactile.

Their affection — rendered as a growing flower — is the sum of your preparation. Afterward, you trim that flower. How well you cut determines how long it lives in your apartment.

When your date arrives, they notice everything. They judge your outfit at the door, react to the drink you pour, wander the living room reading your shelves and reacting to your plants.

Over seven days, the calendar fills. Dates accumulate. Some of them go very well. Those are the ones who stop calling back.

02 — Core Loop

One day. Four phases.
One flower.

  1. Morning
    Newspaper Arrives
    • Read personal ads — scan for highlighted keywords
    • Choose tonight's date
  2. Afternoon
    Preparation Phase — Timed
    • Clean stains, arrange objects, water plants
    • Select vinyl, spray perfume, prepare drinks
    • Set the apartment exactly how you want it seen
  3. Evening
    The Date Arrives
    • Phase 1 — Entrance: outfit, perfume, greeting, cleanliness judged
    • Phase 2 — Kitchen: make their drink from memory or guesswork
    • Phase 3 — Living Room: they investigate everything you placed
  4. Night
    Aftermath
    • Date ends — flower left behind
    • Flower trimming scene: cut stems, score, spawn living plant
    • Sleep — next day begins

    If the date went very well — a souvenir appears the next morning.
    They don't call back.

03 — Key Mechanics

Six interlocking systems
that share one emotional register.

01
Physical Apartment Preparation

Every object in the apartment is a physics prop. You pick things up, carry them, place them. The watering can needs to be filled and tilted. The vinyl record must be extracted from its sleeve and walked to the turntable. Drink bottles pour in real time with layered liquid physics. The preparation phase is not a menu — it is the game.

02
The Date's Gaze

Your date notices specific things in a specific order. Phase 3 is an item-by-item reveal: the date walks to each object in the apartment, pauses, reacts. A thought bubble — heart, neutral face, or frown — appears while a multiplier popup shows how much the item mattered. Items in prominent spots are worth more. The camera auto-pushes in on each discovery.

03
The Flower as Score

Every successful date leaves a flower. You trim it in a standalone scene using virtual scissors — cutting angle, stem length, and condition all feed into a score. That score determines how many days the trimmed flower survives as a living plant in your apartment. A bad trim leaves clippings on the floor and a plant that browns within days.

04
The Keyword System

Every character has preferences that surface as highlighted keywords across the newspaper, dialogue, and item descriptions.

jazz terracotta bookbinding fermentation synthetic clutter

The system rewards close reading. Attention paid to a personal ad on day one pays off in affection on day three.

05
The Mood Machine

Weather, time of day, music, plant health, and air quality all feed into a single 0–1 mood value. This drives the directional light color, ambient tone, fog density, rain particles, and audio mix in real time. A stormy evening with a dead plant and no music feels different from a clear sunset with jazz and a healthy fern. The date notices.

06
The Horror Layer — Implicit

Dates who score very high affection — the great ones, the ones you prepared perfectly for — vanish after that night. They stop appearing in the newspaper. Calls go unanswered. A personal item of theirs appears in the apartment the next morning: a necklace on the counter, a ring in the kitchen drawer. These souvenirs accumulate. Future dates notice them. The game never explains Nema. The player connects the dots.

04 — Aesthetic and Tone

Cozy on the surface.
A slow accumulation of dread beneath it.

Visual Style

PS2-era low-poly with warm URP rendering. Soft vertex snapping, affine texture warping, and 2D dithering give the game a distinctly retro texture while the lighting and color grading remain modern and intentional. Time-of-day shifts from morning blue-white to golden late-afternoon to cool night through a single shader-driven sky system.

Palette & Tone

Muted, warm. Cream walls, terracotta pots, soft greens, amber lamplight. The PSX aesthetic keeps the game feeling hand-made rather than procedural. Every surface reads as a deliberate choice. The horror is environmental and never explicit — players who pay close attention will understand what Nema is. Players who don't will have a pleasant apartment sim.

05 — Target Audience

Two audiences.
One game that delivers for both.

Primary

Cozy game players who want more mechanical depth. Fans of apartment sims, slice-of-life games, and games where preparation and ritual are the gameplay.

Secondary

Horror-adjacent players drawn to psychological dread and unreliable-narrator storytelling. Players who discovered games like Yume Nikki, LSD: Dream Emulator, or Omori through word-of-mouth.

06 — Why This Game

Five things Iris does
that no other game does.

  • The preparation IS the game. Not a menu between story beats — a tactile, physical ritual you perform before every date. The hours spent arranging the apartment are the hours the game is most itself.
  • A date who sees everything. The judgment phase rewards players who paid attention to keyword hints. Every item in the apartment has a potential meaning. Observation is the core skill.
  • Cozy and unsettling in the same scene. The horror is never delivered by a cutscene or a jump scare. It emerges from the same systems the cozy gameplay runs on. The mechanics are the dread.
  • The flower as a living consequence. How you trim your date's flower becomes part of the apartment for days. A bad cut changes the smell, the mood, the mess. Your carefulness persists visually.
  • Fully physical UI. Drinks pour in layered cross-sections. Watering shows a 2D soil-and-water simulation. The record player requires physical handling. Every mechanic has a visual body.

Premium. Finite. Intentional.

Distribution

The game has a defined arc and a deliberate ending. A subscription or live-service model would undermine the tone entirely. The horror layer only works once per player in its intended form — replayability comes from date variety and discovery, not from grinding content that never ends. One-time purchase. No DLC planned. PC (Windows, Mac) at launch; console ports considered post-release based on reception.
