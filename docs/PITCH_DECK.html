01 — Concept

A contemplative life sim about
preparation and consequence.

Spent Blooms is a contemplative life sim about preparing for dates and living with the consequences. You play as Nema, alone in a small apartment. Each morning a newspaper arrives with personal ads. You choose someone to invite over, then spend the day making the apartment worthy of them — wiping stains, arranging objects, choosing music, watering plants, pouring drinks. Every choice is physical, unhurried, and tactile.

Their affection — rendered as a growing flower — is the sum of your preparation. Afterward, you trim that flower. The trimmed bloom joins a growing bouquet in your refrigerator.

When your date arrives, they notice everything. They judge your outfit at the door, react to the drink you pour, wander the living room reading your shelves and reacting to your plants.

Over seven days, the calendar fills. Dates accumulate. Some of them go very well. Those are the ones who stop calling back.

03 — Core Loop

One day. Four phases.
One flower.

  1. Morning
    Newspaper Arrives
    • Read personal ads — scan for highlighted keywords
    • Choose tonight's date
  2. Afternoon
    Preparation Phase — Timed
    • Clean stains, arrange objects, water plants
    • Select vinyl, spray perfume, prepare drinks
    • Set the apartment exactly how you want it seen
  3. Evening
    The Date Arrives
    • Phase 1 — Entrance: outfit, perfume, greeting, cleanliness judged
    • Phase 2 — Kitchen: make their drink from memory or guesswork
    • Phase 3 — Living Room: they investigate everything you placed
  4. Night
    The Aftermath
    • Date ends — a flower is left behind
    • Flower trimming scene: cut the stems, score the arrangement
    • Trimmed flower joins the growing bouquet in the fridge
    • Sleep — next morning begins

    Each person visits three times. After the third date — the one with the strange flower — they don't call back. A souvenir appears the next morning.

04 — Key Mechanics

Six interlocking systems
that share one emotional register.

01
Physical Apartment Preparation

Every object in the apartment is a physics prop. You pick things up, carry them, place them. The watering can needs to be filled and tilted. The vinyl record must be extracted from its sleeve and walked to the turntable. Drink bottles pour in real time with layered liquid physics. The preparation phase is not a menu — it is the game.

02
The Date's Gaze

Your date notices specific things in a specific order. Phase 3 is an item-by-item reveal: the date walks to each object, pauses, reacts. A thought bubble — heart, neutral face, or frown — appears while a multiplier popup shows how much the item mattered. Items in prominent spots are worth more.

03
The Flower as Score

Every date leaves a flower. You trim it in a standalone scene using virtual scissors — cutting angle, stem length, and condition all feed into a score. The trimmed bloom joins a growing bouquet in the refrigerator — a physical record of every date you've had. Each person brings three flowers across three dates. The first two are beautiful. The third one is... different.

04
The Keyword System

Every character has preferences that surface as highlighted keywords across the newspaper, dialogue, and item descriptions.

jazz terracotta bookbinding fermentation synthetic clutter

The system rewards close reading. Attention paid to a personal ad on day one pays off in affection on day three.

05
The Mood Machine

Weather, time of day, music, plant health, and air quality all feed into a single 0–1 mood value. This drives light color, ambient tone, fog density, rain particles, and audio mix in real time. A stormy evening with a dead plant and no music feels different from a clear sunset with jazz and a healthy fern. The date notices.

06
The Horror Layer — Woven In, Never Explained

Each person visits three times. You learn their preferences, refine your preparation, deepen the romance. Their third flower arrives wrong — wilted, dark, thorned. After that final date, they vanish. They stop appearing in the newspaper. Calls go unanswered. A personal item appears in the apartment: a necklace on the counter, a ring in the kitchen drawer. Every player will notice people stop showing up. The horror isn't hidden — it's unavoidable. What's optional is how deeply you read into it. The game never explains Nema. The player decides what it means.

05 — Aesthetic & Tone

Cozy first. Mystery always.
Dread only when it counts.

Visual Style

PS2-era low-poly with warm URP rendering. Soft vertex snapping, affine texture warping, and 2D dithering give the game a distinctly retro texture while the lighting and color grading remain modern and intentional. Time-of-day shifts from morning blue-white to golden late-afternoon to cool night through a single shader-driven sky system.

Tone Balance
60% COZY 25% MYSTERY & FUN 15% CREEPY & SEXY

The apartment sim is genuine. The observation layer is playful detective work. The romance lingers. The rare moments of dread land hard because the rest of the game earned your trust.

Audio Identity

The record player is a core game object — vinyl selection directly affects the apartment's mood. Original lo-fi and jazz tracks scored for each in-game record. Ambient audio shifts with weather and time of day. Sound design is tactile: the click of a bottle cap, water filling a can, scissors on a stem. Every interaction sounds as physical as it looks.

Palette

Muted, warm. Cream walls, terracotta pots, soft greens, amber lamplight. The PSX aesthetic keeps the game feeling hand-made rather than procedural. Every surface reads as a deliberate choice.

Horror-adjacent and romance players drawn to psychological dread, romantic tension, and unreliable-narrator storytelling. Players who found Doki Doki Literature Club, Omori, or Boyfriend Dungeon through word-of-mouth.

Content Creators

The hidden horror layer creates a natural "reaction video" pipeline — the same dual-discovery mechanic that made DDLC a cultural event with 11M+ downloads.

Market position: Steam's cozy/life-sim tag has grown 40%+ year-over-year in revenue since 2022. Top cozy indie releases averaged well over 500K copies. Spent Blooms targets the intersection of three growing audiences — cozy, dating, and horror — that no current title occupies.

07 — Why This Game

Five things Spent Blooms does
that no other game does.

  • The preparation IS the game. Not a menu between story beats — a tactile, physical ritual you perform before every date. The hours spent arranging the apartment are the hours the game is most itself.
  • A date who sees everything. The judgment phase rewards players who paid attention to keyword hints. Every item in the apartment has potential meaning. Observation is the core skill.
  • Cozy, romantic, and unsettling in the same scene. The horror is never delivered by a cutscene or a jump scare. It emerges from the same systems the cozy gameplay and romance run on. The mechanics are the dread.
  • The bouquet as a living record. Every trimmed flower joins a growing bouquet in the refrigerator. Open the fridge and your dating history stares back at you — beautiful stems beside wilted ones, strange third flowers next to perfect first dates.
  • Two marketing cycles from one game. Launch sells a cozy apartment dating sim. Post-launch discovery of the horror layer drives a second wave of organic content creator coverage — the same pattern that made Doki Doki Literature Club a phenomenon.
